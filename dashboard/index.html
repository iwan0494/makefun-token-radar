The answer, before the board

Three ways to be wrong, and how wrong the fit is on each

A cover line fitted on 81 traded tokens can always be made to look right on those same 81. These three columns refuse that: each prices tokens the line did not fit on. The headline is the median miss — how far, in multiples, a priced cover lands from the cover the token went on to record. Lower is better; within-3× is the share of reads that land inside a factor of three.

    What each term is worth, by taking it away

    Leave-one-out again, with one term held out at a time. A term worth keeping pushes the median miss up when it leaves — the bar runs right. A term that changes nothing is a term the fit should not charge for.

      What this does not claim. Nothing here predicts whether or when a protocol issues a token. The fit prices a record at one stated horizon from the market’s own answer for tokens of the same size, family and shape; the held-out figures are the fit’s error on tokens it forgot, not a promise on any protocol. A protocol with no token on record may still carry one the snapshot does not hold. Nothing is investment advice, and every plan rests on the assumptions printed in its own arithmetic.
